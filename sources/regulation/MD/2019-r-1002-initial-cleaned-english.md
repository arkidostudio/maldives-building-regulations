# Regulation on Building in Islands or Areas Without Planning Rules

## CHAPTER ONE — Preliminary

### 1. Introduction and Title
(a) This regulation is made under Section 95(a)(12) of the Construction Act (Act No. 4/2017) to prescribe the procedure for building in islands or areas where no planning rules are in force.
(b) This regulation shall be cited as the **"Regulation on Building in Islands or Areas Without Planning Rules."**

### 2. Purpose
The purpose of this regulation is to:
(a) Set criteria for determining the height of buildings in islands or areas where no planning rules are in force.
(b) Facilitate the construction of safe and healthy buildings in islands or areas where no planning rules are in force.
(c) Describe the measures to be taken, and identify the responsible enforcement authorities, for persons who violate this regulation.

### 3. Enforcement Authority
This regulation shall be enforced by the Government Ministry entrusted with implementing construction-related policies, and by the agencies designated by the Ministry to issue construction permits.

### 4. Effective Date
This regulation shall come into force 60 (sixty) days after its publication in the Government Gazette.

### 5. Definitions
Unless the context otherwise requires, the following terms have the meanings set out below:

- **"Ministry"** — the Government Ministry entrusted with implementing the relevant policies.
- **"Minister"** — the Minister responsible for the Ministry entrusted with implementing construction policies.
- **"Island"** — the area from the shoreline (creek) to the outskirts of the island, and the areas around the island and its hills.
- **"City"** — a town or administrative area designated as a city by the Government.
- **"Development Control"** — the planning standards for the site under construction.
- **"Land"** — state (Bandaara) land and private land.
- **"Road"** — the main road, street, or lane adjoining the building, or the road leading to the subdivided plot.
- **"Road width"** — the width of the road from the front of the permitted building site to the other side of the road, up to the street boundary of the land or premises.
- **"Building footprint"** — the ground-floor area of the land, excluding vacant space left unbuilt on the ground floor.
- **"Building width coefficient"** — the exact outside length of the building divided into the building area:

  Width coefficient = (1 / maximum exact length) × Area

  (1) Calculations in this Article shall be made in metres, and the building area shall be expressed in square metres.
  (2) If the width coefficient cannot be obtained due to the dimensions of a plot or premises, the width coefficient and the maximum permissible building height shall be determined by the Ministry.
- **"Opening"** — openings such as windows and vents. Glass blocks used solely for lighting, non-opening glass, drawers, and sliding doors/windows are not included.
- **"Building Code"** — the standards to be observed in construction.
- **"Compliance Document"** — the documents containing the rules and standards for construction under the Building Code.
- **"Ramp"** — a wooden (or similar) surface used to ascend and descend from a raised level.
- **"Construction Act" / "Act"** — Act No. 4/2017 (Building Act) and its amendments.

## CHAPTER TWO — Construction Practice

### 6. Design of the Building (Development Control)
(a) Except for buildings constructed on land allocated for residential purposes in accordance with this regulation, buildings shall be designed as described in this Chapter, and only after obtaining development control from the designated development controller at the locations specified by the Ministry. This applies to:
  (1) buildings exceeding 18.5 m in height on residential land in residential areas *other than* those designated as cities;
  (2) buildings exceeding 30.48 m in height on residential land in areas designated as cities;
  (3) construction on land allocated by the Government for various development purposes;
  (4) construction on land designated as an industrial zone or economic zone;
  (5) construction in islands other than residential islands;
  (6) construction on farms.

(b) The development control referred to in (a) shall address building height, vacant space, and parking standards.

(c) Notwithstanding subsection (a)(5), a single-storey structure roofed and not exceeding 6 m in height, on a footprint not exceeding 1,200 sq ft, built to maintain premises in islands allocated for non-residential purposes, is not required to comply with the development control prescribed by the Ministry.

### 7. Other Construction
(a) Buildings on the premises referred to in Article 6(a) shall be constructed in accordance with the planning standards prescribed in this regulation.
(b) Where the development control referred to in Article 6 specifies rules and standards to be observed, those standards shall be observed in the design of the building as described in the relevant regulations.

## CHAPTER THREE — Height of Buildings on Residential Land

### 8. Height of Buildings on Residential Land in Non-City Areas
For buildings on land allocated for residential purposes in islands other than those designated as cities, the height from the yard to the roof beam / terrace slab / deck shall be determined as follows:

(a) If the plot area is less than 100 sq ft, or the building footprint is narrower than 1.829 m (6 ft), no building may exceed 1 storey or 6 m in height.
(b) If the footprint is less than 600 sq ft, the maximum height shall be 10.5 m (34.449 ft).
(c) If the footprint is 600 sq ft or more, the maximum height shall be 18.5 m (54.134 ft).

### 9. Height of Buildings on Residential Land in Cities
For buildings on residential land in areas designated as cities, the height from the ground to the top of the roof beam / terrace slab / deck shall be determined as follows:

(a) If the footprint is less than 100 sq ft, or the width coefficient is less than 1.829 m (6 ft), no building higher than 1 storey may be built.

(b) If the footprint is less than 800 sq ft, the maximum height shall be the *lower* of the heights calculated based on (i) the width coefficient of the building, and (ii) the width of the access road.

(c) Where the width coefficient of the building is less than seven-tenths of the square root of the building's footprint area, the maximum height is determined from the width coefficient. (See **Table 1**.) Formula:

  Maximum buildable height = (Width coefficient × 0.3875 + 1.2) / 0.7

(d) Where the square root of the footprint area is greater than or equal to seven-tenths of the width coefficient, the maximum height is determined from the footprint. (See **Table 2**.) Formula:

  Maximum buildable height = Building footprint × 0.3875 + 1.2

(e) The height shall also be determined by the width of the access road to the building, as follows:
  (1) If the road/alley is less than 1.219 m (4 ft) wide → not more than 1 storey and not higher than 6 m.
  (2) Road width 1.219 m (4 ft) to less than 1.524 m (5 ft) → not higher than 9 m (31.53 ft).
  (3) Road width 1.524 m (5 ft) to less than 1.829 m (6 ft) → not higher than 19.20 m (62.99 ft).
  (4) Road width 1.829 m (6 ft) to less than 3.048 m (10 ft) → not higher than 25.20 m (82.68 ft).
  (5) Road width 3.048 m (10 ft) or wider → not higher than 30.48 m (100 ft).

(f) Where the footprint exceeds 800 sq ft, the maximum height shall be determined by considering both the width of the access road and the width coefficient, using the lower value.

(g) In the case referred to in (c), the width coefficient is applied as follows:
  (1) Width coefficient less than 3.0 → not exceeding 16.5 m (54.13 ft).
  (2) Width coefficient greater than 3.0 → the maximum height may not exceed 30.48 m (100 ft).

(h) The maximum height based on access-road width shall be determined in accordance with (e) of this Article.

### 10. Construction Standard Above the Roof Beam / Terrace Slab / Deck
After determining the maximum height under Articles 8 and 9, additional construction above the roof beam / terrace slab / deck on residential-purpose plots shall meet the following:

(a) The staircase enclosure and lift machine-room shelter may be built to a height not exceeding 4 m. The manufacturer's specification for the lift shall be submitted as required in **Schedule 1**.
(b) The staircase enclosure and lift machine-room shelter may be raised up to 1 m (3.280 ft) above the roof. (See Schedule 1.)
(c) Additional roofing may be provided up to 4.921 ft above the roof beam. (See **Table 1**.)
(d) Up to two-thirds of the terrace area may be used for construction, and this section's roof may extend 0.457 m (1.499 ft) into the terrace area.
(e) The height of the lift machine-room section referred to in (d) shall not exceed 4 m.
(f) Antennas and satellite dishes installed for satellite communication are not counted toward the building's height. Antennas and satellite dishes on buildings near an international airport shall be installed in accordance with the relevant regulations of the responsible government agency.

### 11. Raising of the Ground Floor
(a) The ground floor of a building on residential-purpose land shall be up to 600 mm above the top layer of the ground or the courtyard. The floor may be raised, and where it is, the overall permissible building height increases by the same amount, up to a maximum of 600 mm.
(b) Where basement parking is provided on a residential plot and the ground floor is raised above the road's top ground layer or the yard, the raised level shall permit the entire building to be raised by up to 1.5 m in total height.

### 12. Floor-to-Ceiling Height
(a) In residential buildings, at least 2.7 m (8.859 ft) from each floor to the underside of the slab/deck above.
(b) In buildings used for non-residential purposes, at least 2.5 m (8.202 ft) from each floor to the underside of the slab/deck above.

### 13. Road Width Used for Elevation
(a) In determining the height allowed for a subdivided plot, the road width used shall be that of the main road on which the plot is located.
(b) Where a plot is on a street accessible from only one side (because the other side is truncated), the width used shall be that of the main street.

### 14. Where Height Cannot Be Determined
Where the height of a building cannot be determined due to an irregular plot shape or other reasons, the maximum permissible height shall be submitted to the Ministry for determination.

## CHAPTER FOUR — Vacant Space on Residential Land

### 15. Allocation of Vacant Space
(a) If the footprint of a building on residential land is 1,200 sq ft or more, 10% of the footprint shall be allocated as vacant space.
(b) No part of the building may extend into the vacant space referred to in (a), except:
  (1) Roof drainage up to 0.2 m (0.656 ft) from the wall of the building.
  (2) Door/window shutters providing ventilation and light may open into it.
(c) The vacant space may be used for parking.

## CHAPTER FIVE — Building Projections on Residential Land

### 16. Projections Beyond the Road Boundary
No part of a building on residential land may project beyond the road boundary, except:
(a) If the road is less than 6 ft wide: at a height of 2.743 m (9 ft) or more above the road, the roof of the building may project up to 0.2 m (0.656 ft) into the road.
(b) If the road is wider than 6 ft: at a height of 2.743 m (9 ft) or more above the road, the roof may project up to 0.457 m (1.499 ft).

### 17. Doors, Windows and Fanlights Opening Toward the Street
Doors, windows and fanlights that open outward toward the road/boundary shall follow:
(a) They shall not project into the road/boundary at less than 2.743 m (9 ft) above the road.
(b) At heights of 2.743 m (9 ft) or more above the entrance, they may project no more than 0.457 m (1.499 ft) outward.

## CHAPTER SIX — Ventilation and Lighting on Residential Land

### 18. Ventilation and Light to Parts of the Building
Until the relevant Building Code compliance document is published and in force, buildings on residential plots shall be designed to provide ventilation and light to their parts as follows:

(a) Each part of the building shall have an opening of not less than 10% of that part's floor area, facing an unbuilt vacant space.
(b) An opening is deemed to admit airflow to a distance of 6 m from the opening. If the opening area is not less than 15% of the floor area, ventilation is deemed adequate for 4 m per side.
(c) An unbuilt vacant space shall be at least 600 × 600 mm without obstruction. Where such a space is adjacent to another plot, it shall be at least 750 mm from the boundary.
(d) No door, window, fanlight, opening, or fixed glass of any part of a building constructed adjacent to a land boundary shall be placed in the wall abutting the boundary. Any opening or cut shall be set back at least 750 mm from the immediate side of the adjoining plot.
(e) In place of an unbuilt vacant space, the adjacent road or alley may be used. However, where a plot is subdivided, openings on buildings on plots *other than* the one taken from the subdivision are not permitted.
(f) Notwithstanding (e), if the street becomes a government street, it shall be treated the same as an adjacent street to the land.
(g) For non-residential use on only 2 floors of a building (total height not exceeding 7.5 m), and where mechanical ventilation is adequate, the building may be constructed without the spaces and openings described in (a), (b) and (c). The mechanically ventilated space shall extend from its beginning to the top and shall never be on the lowest floor. If the 2 floors are used for residential purposes, openings and spaces shall be provided for ventilation and light as described in (a) and (c).

## CHAPTER SEVEN — Railings (Chains) and Windows on Residential Plots

### 19. Standards for Balconies, Stairs and Windows
Until the compliance documents under the Building Code come into force, the balconies, stairs and windows of buildings shall meet the following:

(a) Railings or walls on balconies and terraces shall be at least 1.2 m (3.94 ft) high. Balconies shall be designed or built with railings or walls designed so a child cannot climb them, and with gaps no greater than 125 mm in any dimension.
(b) Where a balcony or terrace on a building sits adjacent to the boundary of an adjoining plot, a wall of at least 2 m (6.562 ft), or an equivalent barrier, shall be erected.
(c) Railings (chains) or walls as in (a) shall also be installed on balconies and terraces not covered by (b).
(d) Stairs shall be built with railings (chains) or walls at least 850 mm high, designed to be safe for children.
(e) Windows installed in the building at a height of 2.743 m (9 ft) or more above the ground shall be at least 1 m (3.281 ft) above the floor.
(f) Notwithstanding (e), windows opening onto terraces and balconies may be installed more than 1 m below the floor.
(g) If an opening installed in common areas of the building more than 2.743 m (9 ft) above the ground can pass a 125 mm sphere through it, that opening shall be located at least 1 m (3.281 ft) above the floor.

## CHAPTER EIGHT — Parking on Residential Land

### 20. Determination of Parking
Parking space for buildings on residential land shall be determined as follows:
(a) If the footprint exceeds 1,200 sq ft and the building is higher than 5 storeys, at least 10% of the footprint shall be reserved for parking.
(b) Parking on the ground floor of the building is not compulsory, but easy vehicle access shall be provided.
(c) The parking area may be divided into sections, but no section may be less than 5% of the footprint (otherwise it is not counted).
(d) Parking space shall not be allocated when constructing plots on prohibited roads.

## CHAPTER NINE — Miscellaneous

### 21. Garbage Disposal Space
(a) If the footprint is 1,000 sq ft or more, at least 1.5% of the footprint shall be designated as waste disposal space.
(b) Any such space shall be not less than 2 × 2 m.

### 22. Subdivision of Land
Where a residential plot is subdivided by including a street, that street shall be at least 4 ft wide.

### 23. Space Adjacent to Boundaries
Where a building or part of a building is constructed on residential land other than a row/street house, a vacant space of at least 750 mm wide shall be provided along the boundaries. Such 750 mm spaces need not be provided on every side, but the spaces provided shall be accessible for cleaning.

### 24. Non-Residential Uses on Residential Land
A procedure for applying for permits to construct sai-hotels, restaurants, cafés, bakeries, workshops (carpentry etc.), warehouses, and other places of economic activity on residential land shall be published by the Ministry within 50 days.

### 25. Planning Committee
(a) The Minister shall constitute a committee called the **Planning Committee** to decide on any matter not specified in these rules or the Construction Act and falling outside their jurisdiction.
(b) The Committee shall determine its own rules of operation, under (a).
(c) The Planning Committee shall set planning standards and operate under the Ministry.
(d) Where a policy decision is required on matters submitted to the Planning Committee, it shall be taken on the direction of the Minister.
(e) The decisions of the Planning Committee referred to in these rules shall be followed.

## CHAPTER TEN — Action for Violations

### 26. Procedure for Violations
(a) It is prohibited to leave the provisions of these rules unenforced. Any person noticing a significant violation shall notify the building-permit issuing authority.
(b) Upon notification of a violation to a building-permit authority under (a), that authority shall take action and refer the matter to the Ministry (if it does not act) within 30 days.
(c) If a building-permit authority receives information under (a), or itself finds a violation, it shall inspect whether the premises comply with this regulation.
(d) Where an inspection under (c) determines that the premises are non-compliant, or where the authority determines the premises are in a dangerous location or may become so, the site shall be treated as follows:
  (1) The authority shall, under Article 27, notify the person to rectify the matters within a prescribed period.
  (2) The notice under (1) shall state the reason and correction period, and shall be dated, signed and stamped.
  (3) The condition of the premises shall be monitored by the licensing authority for the purposes of paragraph (1).

### 27. Action Against Violators
Each violation of this regulation is subject to separate action as follows:
(a) A fine of MVR 100,000 (One Lakh) shall be imposed separately for each violation, together with a period to rectify the matter, based on the seriousness of the matter.
(b) If the matter is not rectified within the period given under (a), 3% of the fine imposed under (a) shall be added for each additional day the matter remains unrectified.
(c) If the fines payable under (a) and (b) have not been paid and the matter has not been rectified, the case shall be referred to the Attorney General's Office for recovery of the outstanding amount.
